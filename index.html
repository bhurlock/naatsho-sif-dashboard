Methods, definitions and how to cite

What this is

A national log of significant injuries and fatalities (SIFs) and near misses among workers of state departments of transportation and state toll, turnpike, thruway and bridge authorities, in all 50 states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico. Contractors working on those agencies' projects are included and shown as their own group. NAATSHO built it because no national database of these events exists. It is maintained with Claude, an AI system made by Anthropic, under the direction of NAATSHO's database owner, and is checked by state DOT safety professionals.

Who is counted

Included: on-duty events, medical events on the job, and travel or commuting in a state vehicle. Excluded: municipal and county workers, utility crews, tow operators, police and motorists. Suicides and other mental health events are never searched for and are not shown on this page.

How incidents are found

  • Four times a day, a check reads news alerts and searches public news and official sources for new incidents from the past 48 hours.
  • A state-by-state search covers 2015 to the present. Each state's search depth is shown on the map and in the downloaded file: one pass, one pass plus research leads checked against their sources, or a second, year-by-year pass that also opens official records.
  • Leads from other research tools are never counted until a source has been opened and checked.
  • State safety offices confirm or correct records with the Verify or correct form, and can send their own records with the Submit state data form. Changes from a government email address are applied automatically.

Severity

Interim definitions, to be aligned with NCHRP Project 20-24(157) when it is final. Fatal: a work-related death, including a later death from the injuries. Significant: in-patient hospital admission, amputation, loss of an eye, airlifted, or injuries reported as serious, critical or life-threatening. SIFs are Fatal plus Significant. Minor (treated and released, or reported minor), Near miss (no injury but clear potential for a serious one) and Injury, severity not stated (taken to a hospital with no further detail) are shown separately and never added to SIF figures. Being taken to a hospital is not treated as an admission. The definitions follow OSHA's severe-injury reporting rule (29 CFR 1904.39) for consistency, but a case counts even where OSHA reporting would not apply, such as a crash on a public highway.

Confirmation

Confirmed: an official source names the worker's employer, or the agency has verified the record. Probable: news reporting names the employer, with no official confirmation. Incidents where no source names the employer, or names the state agency whose project it was, are held in a review queue and not counted until one does.

How counts work

Every count is workers, not incidents. One crash that injures three workers counts three. Each figure is shown three ways: all workers, state employees, and contractors. Workers whose employer group is not yet known appear in the all-workers figure only. Rates per 10,000 use each state's state-government highway workforce from the Census Annual Survey of Public Employment and Payroll, cover state employees only, and are a floor, because counts are not complete. They are not for ranking states.

Names

Names appear only for workers who died in 2026 or later and only after the name has been publicly released. Names of injured workers are never recorded.

Limits

These counts reflect public reporting and are not complete. Coverage is uneven: states differ in how much is published and in how deeply they have been searched, and contractor incidents and injuries are reported less often than deaths of state employees. Time of day, day of week and season show when incidents were reported, not how much work was under way. Differences between states, regions, years or worker groups should not be read as trends where coverage could explain them.

Refresh

The page is rebuilt from the database each morning at about 5:15 AM Eastern. An incident found later in the day appears the next morning. A notice appears when the data is more than two days old.

Downloading the data

Download incidents (CSV) saves every incident in the current view, with the same fields this page shows plus the state's search depth and the data date. Set the filters first to narrow the file. Internal notes, charge details and recipient information are not included.

How to cite

Questions and corrections

Use the Report or correct an incident form at the top of this page. Each incident also has its own Verify or correct link.

Definitions. Fatal: a work-related death, including a later death from the injuries. Significant: in-patient admission, amputation, loss of an eye, airlifted, or injuries reported as serious, critical or life-threatening. SIF is Fatal plus Significant. Minor injuries, near misses and injuries whose severity was not stated are shown separately and never added to SIF figures. Confirmed means an official source names the employer; Probable means news reporting does.

Limits. This log captures publicly reported incidents and is not complete. Most states have had one search pass, so a low count can mean little was found, not that little happened. Do not read differences between states, regions, years or worker groups as trends where uneven coverage could explain them. Names appear only for workers who died in 2026 or later and whose names were publicly released. Mental health events are not part of this log.

Outlook and recommended actions. These two sections interpret the record and can be switched off. The outlook shows what recent logged history implies if incidents and press coverage both continue at recent levels. It predicts no event and extends no trend line. The actions come from the NAATSHO action library, matched to the leading patterns in the current view. They are considerations drawn from public facts, not findings of cause or fault.
